Deepak Gupta

PhD researcher, Department of Computational and Data Sciences Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore

I work on quantum machine learning for scientific computing: how variational quantum circuits, wavelet bases and physics-informed training can be combined to solve differential equations whose solutions change sharply over small regions, and what such methods cost compared with classical solvers.

I am in the joint M.Tech–Ph.D. programme at IISc, advised by Dr. Ratikanta Behera. My training is in mathematics, and most of my work moves between a formulation on paper, an algorithm, and a circuit simulation that tests it.

Research

One question runs through my work: can quantum circuits represent solutions with sharp, localized, multiscale structure accurately and economically? I approach it through three connected lines of work, from public preprints to a direction I have only begun to explore.

  • Public preprint
  • Manuscript under review

  • Wavelet bases in quantum PINNs

    Preprint, 2025 Follow-up under review

    Physics-informed quantum neural networks inherit the difficulty classical PINNs have with steep gradients and high frequencies. In WPIQNN the quantum circuit produces the coefficients of a wavelet expansion of the solution, so the multiresolution basis carries the local detail, and the PDE residual is assembled from precomputed wavelet derivative matrices instead of automatic differentiation. ER-WPIQNN, a follow-up now under review, prunes the wavelet dictionary with an energy criterion after a first round of training.

    • How it works
    • arXiv:2512.08256

  • Quantum PINNs for nonlocal equations

    Preprint, 2026

    Integro-differential and fractional equations have memory: the solution at a point depends on its history or on the whole domain. We extended quantum PINNs to Volterra-type integro-differential equations, coupled systems and time-fractional integro-PDEs, with two ways of handling the nonlocal terms and an approximation theorem for the circuit class used.

    • How it works
    • arXiv:2606.26865

  • Quantum neural operators

    Exploring

    Neural operators learn maps between function spaces, for example from an initial condition to the solution at a later time, rather than solving one equation at a time. I am exploring whether quantum wavelet transforms can serve as a building block for a quantum counterpart of the wavelet neural operator. This is ongoing work, with no results to report yet.

Papers

  1. arXiv preprint, December 2025

    Wavelet-Accelerated Physics-Informed Quantum Neural Network for Multiscale Partial Differential Equations

    Deepak Gupta, Himanshu Pandey, Ratikanta Behera

    A hybrid quantum–classical PINN whose circuit outputs wavelet coefficients. On heat-conduction, Helmholtz, Klein–Gordon and Maxwell benchmarks it reports lower mean errors than a classical wavelet-based PINN with under 2% of its trainable parameters, in noiseless simulation.

  2. arXiv preprint, June 2026

    Quantum Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Solving Integro and Fractional PDEs

    Deepak Gupta, Ratikanta Behera

    Two quantum PINN variants for nonlocal problems, one using quadrature and one using auxiliary variables, an L2(μ) approximation theorem with rate O(n−1/2) for the circuit class, and experiments on Volterra-type equations and a time-fractional integro-PDE.

  3. Manuscript under review

    ER-WPIQNN: Energy-Refined Wavelet-Based Quantum PINNs for PDEs with Sharp Localized Structures

    A two-stage extension of WPIQNN for solutions with sharp, localized features: train WPIQNN briefly, keep the wavelets that carry the most energy, then refine the model classically on the reduced basis.

Software

Multilevel 2D quantum wavelet transform in PennyLane

I like to understand quantum algorithms by building them. This repository implements the multilevel two-dimensional quantum wavelet transform of Li et al. (IEEE Transactions on Cybernetics) gate by gate in PennyLane: Haar and Daubechies-4 transforms in one and two dimensions, multilevel forward and inverse circuits, and amplitude encoding of images and fields.

Wavelets do for local, multiscale structure what Fourier modes do for global frequencies. Whether quantum wavelet transforms become as useful a building block as the quantum Fourier transform is an open question, and this code is a way to work on it concretely.
